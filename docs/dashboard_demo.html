        🤔 New here? Quick answers

        What am I looking at?

        A pretend farm of 576 small patches. Sensors and drones measure how green (healthy) each patch is, how wet the soil is, and how many pests there are. An AI decides where the drones should go.

        How do the drones avoid crashing into each other?

        Four layers. Each drone has its own launch pad, spaced well apart. Drones flying at the same time cruise at different heights. Before any takeoff, the whole plan is replayed second by second in 3D, and it is rejected if any two drones would ever be both too close sideways and too close vertically (this catches the risky moments of climbing and landing). If a safe overlap can't be found, the drones simply fly one at a time.

        What happens when it is windy?

        Wind changes how much battery a flight uses (a headwind costs a lot, a tailwind saves some), and it is stronger higher up, so the highest flight layer gives up first. Above a safe wind speed nothing flies at all, and spraying has its own lower limit because wind blows the spray off target. Gusty air also makes the drones keep a bigger safe distance from each other. Anything postponed is simply done on a calmer day.

        What if a drone loses its GPS?

        Without GPS a drone cannot find its way home, so it does not try. It instantly stops spraying, waits a few seconds in case the signal comes back, and if not, lands safely right where it is. Any drone flying below it is sent home immediately so nothing can fall on it. The landed drone is picked up later, and its unfinished work is rescheduled.

        Do the drones recharge?

        Yes. Each drone has a battery that is tracked day to day. Between flights it recharges on its pad (or swaps to a spare pack), and overnight everything charges to full. A slow charger means later flights can cover less, so the planner budgets each flight from what the battery can really hold. Batteries also wear out slowly with use.

        Why do drones skip some patches?

        Safety first: they never fly into no-fly zones, never go if the battery wouldn't leave a safe reserve, and each drone flies at its own height so they can't collide.

        What does "AI accuracy" mean?

        How often the AI correctly tells a struggling patch from a healthy one. Because struggling patches are rare, we measure it fairly: it gets credit for catching the sick ones and for not raising false alarms.

        Does it run by itself?

        Yes. A scheduled program runs one "day" automatically, saves what it did, re-trains the AI when conditions change, and writes weekly reports. A human can switch everything off with one setting (the kill switch).

        Is any of this real?

        No. It's a simulation, so numbers like "water saved" show what the approach could do, not proven field results.
